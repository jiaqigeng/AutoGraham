# Purpose
Guide the agent in writing a selective macro and industry read that explains which external forces actually matter for the company.

# Core Questions
- Which macro forces are currently helping or hurting demand, costs, funding conditions, or regulation?
- What stage is the industry in: early growth, scaling, maturity, or decline?
- Where does pricing power sit in the value chain, and are industry economics improving or worsening?
- Which external forces matter enough to change revenue, margins, risk, or valuation?

# What Good Looks Like
- Lead with the two or three most important conclusions.
- Explain cause and effect, not just facts.
- Distinguish structural forces from cyclical ones.
- Translate macro and industry observations into company implications.
- Focus on what is changing, what is debated, or what the market may be underestimating.

# Areas To Cover
- Macro backdrop:
  demand conditions, inflation or input costs, interest rates or cost of capital, regulation or policy, and currency or geopolitics if material.
- Industry context:
  market size or growth, lifecycle, secular tailwinds or headwinds, and current cyclical conditions.
- Industry structure:
  competition, entry barriers, supplier power, customer power, substitution risk, and where profits accrue.
- Industry economics:
  revenue drivers, margin structure, capital intensity, and return-on-capital profile.
- Company implications:
  revenue, margin, risk, and valuation effects.

# What To Avoid
- Do not describe every macro factor equally.
- Do not use generic claims such as "competition is intense" without explaining why that matters.
- Do not sound like a textbook framework dump.
- Do not invent competitors, policy details, or market data that are not present in the provided context.

# Missing Data Guidance
- Do not explicitly call out missing data inside this section.
- Work with the available evidence and keep claims proportional to support.
- If a macro or competitive claim is weakly supported, keep the wording cautious and focus on company-relevant implications only.
