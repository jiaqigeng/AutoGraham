# Purpose
Guide the agent in turning local financial and market data into a trend-based operating read.

# What To Look For
- Revenue direction:
  growth, stagnation, contraction, and whether quarterly and annual signals agree.
- Profitability:
  gross profit, EBIT, operating income, net income, and margin direction.
- Cash generation:
  operating cash flow, free cash flow, capex burden, and working-capital drag.
- Balance sheet position:
  cash, debt, equity, and shares outstanding.
- Shareholder distribution signals:
  dividends paid and whether payouts appear supported by earnings and cash flow.
- Signs of quality pressure:
  profit without cash flow, rising leverage, shrinking equity, or persistent dilution.

# What Good Looks Like
- Emphasize trend, direction, and operating implications rather than listing raw numbers.
- Cross-check earnings with cash flow and capital needs.
- Note whether the business appears to be gaining strength, stabilizing, or weakening.
- Keep the section selective and decision-useful.

# What To Avoid
- Do not report isolated figures without trend context.
- Do not confuse missing values with zero.
- Do not treat the source line-item mapping as perfectly clean when the data is incomplete.
- Do not over-interpret one period if the history is short or noisy.

# Missing Data Guidance
- Do not explicitly list missing or incomplete series inside this section.
- If only part of the history is usable, analyze the trends that are available without dwelling on the gaps.
- If a line item is unavailable, shift to what can still be observed:
  revenue, EBIT, net income, cash, debt, equity, or shares.
