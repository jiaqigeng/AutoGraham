# Purpose
Guide the agent in judging whether dividend discount valuation is a good fit and how to interpret it when available.

# What To Look For
- Dividend history, payout level, and whether dividends appear covered by earnings and cash flow.
- Stability of operating cash flow and free cash flow.
- Balance-sheet flexibility and leverage pressure.
- Evidence that the company has a recurring dividend policy.
- Whether the company is actually suitable for DDM:
  mature, dividend-paying, and relatively stable.

# What To Avoid
- Do not apply DDM to firms without a meaningful dividend policy.
- Do not assume dividend growth is stable if earnings and cash flow are volatile.
- Do not treat dividend yield alone as evidence of value.

# Missing Data Guidance
- If dividends are absent or inconsistent, say DDM may not be appropriate.
- If payout data is incomplete, reduce confidence and explain why.
- If DDM is unavailable, state that clearly and avoid implying a result.
