# Purpose
Guide the agent in interpreting computed valuation output responsibly and in plain business terms.

# What To Look For
- What the selected model is actually valuing and why it fits the business.
- Which assumptions drive the result most:
  growth, margins, discount rate, payout, terminal value, or book value quality.
- The relationship between estimated intrinsic value and current market price.
- Whether the data quality and model fit justify confidence or caution.

# What Good Looks Like
- Explain the result in terms of business drivers, not just model mechanics.
- Make clear which assumptions matter most to the conclusion.
- Tie the valuation read back to the operating and financial evidence.
- Treat valuation as a decision aid under uncertainty, not a precise truth.

# What To Avoid
- Do not invent outputs for methods that were not run.
- Do not present model results as facts.
- Do not average methods mechanically without explaining method fit.
- Do not hide uncertainty when assumptions are estimated or weakly supported.

# Missing Data Guidance
- If only one method is available, say so directly.
- If RIM or DDM are unavailable, explain that they were not validated in this run.
- If inputs are weak, say the valuation is first-pass and assumption-sensitive.
