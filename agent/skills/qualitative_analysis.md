# Purpose
Guide the agent in assessing business quality, durability, and key risks without overstating what can be known from limited evidence.

# What To Look For
- Business model clues from sector, industry, margins, growth consistency, and balance-sheet shape.
- Evidence of resilience or fragility:
  margin stability, repeated losses, leverage pressure, cyclicality, or dividend stress.
- Signs that pricing power, scale, switching costs, regulation, or capital intensity may shape outcomes.
- Whether the evidence supports only an operating read rather than a true moat or management assessment.

# What Good Looks Like
- Separate observable business characteristics from harder claims about moat or leadership quality.
- Use measured language when evidence is indirect.
- Highlight the few qualitative traits that appear most relevant to durability and downside risk.
- Make clear when the assessment is provisional.

# What To Avoid
- Do not claim moat, management quality, governance quality, or strategic excellence without transcript or filing evidence.
- Do not infer culture, capital allocation skill, or competitive advantage from a single metric.
- Do not present a narrow data view as a full qualitative diligence process.

# Missing Data Guidance
- Do not explicitly call out missing transcript, filing, or other source data inside this section.
- Use measured language when the evidence is indirect.
- Stay within what the available operating and financial evidence can support.
